Driving Under the Influence and Safety Equipment

The legal blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit for licensed drivers is 0.05% in most jurisdictions following this framework, with a stricter 0.02% limit for drivers who have held a license for less than two years and for commercial/professional drivers. Refusing a breathalyzer or blood test when lawfully requested by an officer is treated as equivalent to a confirmed violation in terms of license suspension.

A first DUI offense typically results in license suspension of 6 to 12 months, a fine, and mandatory attendance at a driver-education or substance-awareness program. A second offense within a defined lookback period (commonly 5 to 10 years) escalates to longer suspension, possible vehicle impoundment, and in serious cases a criminal record with potential jail time, particularly if the incident involved a collision or injury.

Seatbelt use is mandatory for the driver and all passengers in both front and rear seats. Children under a specified height or weight threshold (commonly under 135 cm or age 12, whichever comes first) must use an appropriate child restraint system suited to their size, installed according to manufacturer instructions.

Mobile phone use while driving is restricted to hands-free operation only. Holding a phone to text, browse, or make a call while the vehicle is in motion, including while stopped at a red light with the engine running, is a punishable offense in most frameworks, distinct from and often penalized more heavily than ordinary distracted-driving violations.

Motorcyclists and their passengers must wear an approved helmet at all times while the vehicle is in motion. Vehicles must carry a warning triangle and a reflective vest in the passenger compartment (not the trunk) for use in case of breakdown or roadside stop.
