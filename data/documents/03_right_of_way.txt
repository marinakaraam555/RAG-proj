Right of Way Rules at Intersections

At an uncontrolled intersection (no signs or signals), the vehicle arriving first has the right of way. If two vehicles arrive simultaneously, the vehicle on the right has priority over the vehicle on the left. This "priority to the right" rule is the default in most jurisdictions unless local signage states otherwise.

At a four-way stop, each driver must come to a complete stop regardless of arrival order. The vehicle that stopped first proceeds first. In cases of simultaneous arrival, the same priority-to-the-right rule applies. Rolling stops, where a vehicle slows but does not fully halt, are a common and heavily enforced violation at these intersections.

Roundabouts give priority to vehicles already circulating inside the roundabout. Entering traffic must yield to circulating traffic regardless of the entering vehicle's position relative to the roundabout. Drivers must signal their exit intention and are not permitted to stop inside a roundabout except to avoid a collision.

Emergency vehicles displaying active lights and sirens have absolute right of way. All other drivers must yield by pulling to the nearest safe curb position and stopping, even if this requires crossing into an oncoming lane briefly or proceeding through a red light after confirming it is safe to do so, in that specific order.

Pedestrians at a marked or unmarked crosswalk have right of way once they have stepped into the roadway with clear intent to cross, provided it is safe for the driver to stop. Turning vehicles must yield to pedestrians crossing the street the vehicle is turning into, even when the driver has a green light.
